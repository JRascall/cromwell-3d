# Study

Notes on how the games this prototype borrows from draw what they draw, written
for understanding the system rather than for dropping into an engine.

The governing idea (§2) is that **the vehicle's geometry *is* its physics model**: parts carry an area, an airfoil and a transform, and every tick the flow is resolved **in each transform's own frame**. So pitching moment is the tailplane's force applied aft; **sideslip needs no term** because a fin is a part rolled 90° and the same `atan2` is its slip angle; flaps are a rewritten wing area; a listing ship is off-centre buoyancy. §4 is the one to read: **control surfaces produce a quaternion and nothing else** — `ControlSurfaceJob → SetLocalRotationJob → ReadTransformJob → AeroJob`, an turns 34° per tick, and **autorotation and torque reaction that are not implemented** — they fall out of `ω += (Q_engine + Q_aero − friction)·dt / I`. §8.4 solves the VTOL lift-fan thrust from a **moment balance about the current centre of mass**, every tick, in six lines. §9 is a hand-written raycast suspension with soft-ground sinkage and a **contact-patch stiction anchor** — the correct fix for parked vehicles creeping. §11 is the ships: **one rigidbody, N compartments as buoyancy probes**, `lerp(ρ_air, ρ_water, submerged)·g·V` for a smooth waterline, anisotropic quadratic drag (lateral ≫ longitudinal — that is the keel), flooding against an **exhaustible damage-control pool** that propagates to neighbours when spent, a hull that slows because its longitudinal drag lerps toward its lateral one, and **death by posture** (below the surface, heeled past 60°, bow up past 14°) rather than by hit points. **The sea is a flat plane and the wake is particles** — §11.3 and §11.7 are the honest negative findings, and the lesson that *a medium's fidelity should be set by what interacts with it*. §12 is the cost answer: hot fields as POD in native memory with the depth and server authority trade against each other directly**. §18 ranks what transfers. **No talk, blog or paper exists**, and §19 says what was not established — including that per-vehicle authored values are unrecoverable because this build ships with script type trees stripped, so §17's numbers are physically derived rather than Shockfront's. |
| [`nuclear_option_combat.md`](games/flight/nuclear_option/nuclear_option_combat.md) | The combat half of [`nuclear_option.md`](games/flight/nuclear_option/nuclear_option.md) — **sensors, guidance and AI flight, and the one idea that ties them together: every counter-tactic in the game attacks a named term in a named equation, and you can point at both.** §1 is the radar: a **closed-form horizon test** (`sqrt(2Rh)` per end, summed) that rejects before the raycast; clutter as `targetRadius²·2 / radarAlt²`, **inverse-square in height above terrain**, subtracted *before* the detection floor; and signal as `maxRange/dist · RCS^0.25`, where the fourth root makes stealth valuable but never decisive. **§1.5 is notching, and it is one dot product** — `min(|dot(losDir, targetVelocity)|, 150) · dopplerFactor` applied as a *multiplier*, so flying abeam does not hide you, it removes your processing gain; you disappear only by stacking it with the clutter term, which is why the AI does both. Plus roll-before-pull, rudder banned above 20° off, idle-throttle rules expressed against corner speed, `sqrt(mass/maxWeight)` approach speed, and §7.5 where the AI computes the notch vector as a **double cross product** — the analytic solution for “minimise that dot product” — descends to 10 m for clutter, and releases chaff *only* once established in the notch. Difficulty is `randomError / skill` on the evasion geometry, so a weak AI flies badly rather than cheating. §8 ranks fourteen transferable ideas and two anti-patterns. |
| [`nuclear_option_command.md`](games/flight/nuclear_option/nuclear_option_command.md) | The third Nuclear Option note — **everything above and beside the single aircraft**, and the observation that ties it together: **there is one world model, one target-scoring function and one reservation mechanism, and every AI shares all three.** §1 is `TrackingInfo`, 90 lines that are the game's entire knowledge model: **fog of war is one timestamp and a four-second grace** (`GetPosition()` returns the truth if seen recently and the frozen memory otherwise, so every consumer inherits it for free), and **multi-unit deconfliction is two `sbyte`s** — `attackers` and `missileAttacks` sit in the denominator of every scoring function, so committing to a target makes it less attractive to everyone else. | [`nuclear_option_control.md`](games/flight/nuclear_option/nuclear_option_control.md) | The fourth Nuclear Option note — **every path from an input to a force**, human or AI, in the air, on the ground, on the water and on foot, plus the wind field that perturbs all of them. Written to close the gaps the other three left, and it lands on two observations. **One: there is exactly one control interface and everything writes to it.** `ControlInputs` is six floats; a stick, a mouse, an AI combat state, an autopilot and a taxi procedure all write it, then it goes through the same fly-by-wire and the same aerodynamics — so the AI cannot cheat, every flight-model improvement improves the AI for free, and a replicated remote player's inputs animate their control surfaces with no separate path. **Two: there is one contact idiom, reused four times** — cast a ray down, spring-damper on the hit normal, righting torque toward the surface, **subtract the surface's own velocity**, clamp tangential force to a friction circle. | [`nuclear_option_audio.md`](games/flight/nuclear_option/nuclear_option_audio.md) | The narrowest Nuclear Option note and the one with the highest ideas-per-line: **how do you make a vehicle sound right at 600 m/s, and a battlefield right when the things making noise are ten kilometres away?** Three separate hard problems, solved with **no audio middleware whatsoever** — no FMOD, no Wwise, no Steam Audio, no reverb, no occlusion — in about 250 lines of stock Unity `AudioSource` and `AudioMixer`. §2 is that negative finding read against [`broken_arrow.md`](games/flight/broken_arrow/broken_arrow.md), which buys *two* audio packages for an easier problem. **§3, Doppler:** sources opt into a per-unit list, and the *camera state* toggles it — the aircraft you are flying or chasing gets `dopplerLevel = 0` and `spatialBlend = 0` (2D, centred), which kills the warble a chase camera's own motion creates *and* is the physically correct answer, since you do not experience Doppler from a source you travel with. Everything else runs at **0.6, not 1.0**, because full-strength Doppler at supersonic closure sounds like a broken tape. Then `JetNozzle` damps it further with one dot product — `max(1 − 2·camFacing, 0.01)`, near-zero within 60° of astern, exactly the geometry of every tail chase — and gets a **4:1 rear-to-front exhaust loudness** out of the same dot product. **§4, distance:** explosions register a *pending* sound on an expanding wavefront (`propagation += 340f * dt`, started at the cube-root fireball radius) and play when the sphere reaches the listener — with the low-pass cutoff set to **`22000 / travelTime`**, one divide that models atmospheric absorption and turns a delayed sound from a *late* one into a *distant* one. | [`broken_arrow_aircraft.md`](games/flight/broken_arrow/broken_arrow_aircraft.md) | **How does an aircraft reach a waypoint accurately enough to bomb with, and what happens when the order changes on short final?** The counterpart to [`nuclear_option_command.md`](games/flight/nuclear_option/nuclear_option_command.md) §6–8, and the two answer it at **opposite poles for a structural reason**. | [`broken_arrow_squads.md`](games/flight/broken_arrow/broken_arrow_squads.md) | **How do you run infantry at RTS scale without simulating nine men?** Read from the same retail Il2CppDumper dump as the aircraft note, and the answer is a **three-tier entity model** where almost every property falls out of *where a decision was placed*. **The squad is the unit** — one path, one `HealthComponent`, one `BoxCollider`, one set of eyes, one avoidance agent, one FMOD instance — and every battle system touches it with **the identical code that drives a tank**. **The soldier is a body**: a real entity, but with no health, no collider, no path, no vision and no target; its entire combat state is `IsAiming` and which of two guns is in its hands. | [`surface_depth.md`](topics/surfaces/surface_depth.md) | Why a **Megascans** surface in Unreal reads as having real relief when it is a flat polygon — read from Epic's engine source. Four things stack, and **the biggest is the data**: scanned normals carry real micro-structure rather than being derived from a height map, the **AO/cavity maps are baked from real geometry** so crevices darken correctly — which is one of the strongest depth cues there is — and the albedo is de-lit so baked lighting doesn't fight the runtime. *If the maps are wrong no parallax will save the surface; if they're right, a lot of depth appears with none.* Then the ladder, with maths recovered from the compiler: **`BumpOffset`** is `UV + CameraVector_TangentSpace.xy * HeightRatio * (Height − ReferencePlane)` — one sample, pivoting about mid-height, and notably using the *offset-limiting* variant with no divide by view-Z, so it stays stable at grazing angles. **Parallax Occlusion Mapping** (Unreal ships it as a material *function*, not a node — `ParallaxOcclusionMapping.uasset`, plus a `_BoundedUVs` variant and one for texture bombing) ray-marches the height field for genuine occlusion at 8–32 samples. And **Pixel Depth Offset** is the piece most people miss: it makes the material write the depth buffer, clamped by `max(SceneDepth + PDO, SceneDepth)` so it can **only push away, never extrude** — which is why POM makes holes and not bumps — using `SV_DepthLessEqual` to keep early-Z, with Epic's comment documenting a real PS4 `RE_Z` double-depth-test bug that makes the clamp necessary *even when the offset is zero*. Because PDO also appears in the depth-only, velocity and shadow passes, the lesson generalises: **an illusion that exists in only one pass is exposed by every other one.** §4 is the test that settles it — every trick short of real displacement leaves **the silhouette flat**, so choose by whether the outline is visible, not by how much depth you want. §3.5 confirms `NaniteTessellation.ush` is real, table-driven and distance-faded. |

| [`raytracing.md`](plans/raytracing.md) | **A design note, not research** — what ray-traced lighting and shadows would cost, asked with "on par with RE ENGINE" as the bar, and the bar is what dissolves most of the question. Two facts do the work. **OpenGL has no ray tracing at any version and never will** — not a gap but a decision, stated by NVIDIA's own staff (*"Unfortunately, NVIDIA never added Ray Tracing Support to OpenGL"*), with the trap named because a search finds it in two results: **`GL_NV_ray_tracing` is a *GLSL* extension for SPIR-V compiled for Vulkan, not a GL entry point**, and the `GL_` prefix is the shading language's naming convention rather than an API claim. And **RE ENGINE's ray tracing does not include shadows** — no runtime ray-traced shadow exists in v1 or v2 at all, the first is the v3 area-light shadow, and Dragon's Dogma 2 in 2024 says it in one line: *"GI lighting reuses shadow map for shadows. Unable to represent shadows outside field of vision."* So the request, read against its own benchmark, is **ray-traced indirect with rasterised direct shadows** — parity on the shadow half is a better shadow map, and the fix DD2 ships for the frustum wall (**inject the previous frame's ray hits into the shadow map**) needs no RT hardware to imitate. §3 is the prerequisite chain and the point of the note: eight rows, and the only one that is *ray tracing* is the last. **A TLAS is a scene**, so RT is blocked behind [`MIGRATION.md`](../src/cromwell/rhi/MIGRATION.md) §4.12's render scene — you cannot build an acceleration structure from a `submit()` callback, which is the second independent argument for that item after culling and sorting. **The denoiser is half the bill** (Village on PS5: 4.7 ms, of which 2.2 is reconstruction) and this tree has **zero hits for motion vectors, reprojection or any history buffer** — which §6.6 of the RE note currently records as a *virtue*, since the 2× supersample reconstructs from nothing and has no tuning burden; adding RT spends that virtue. §4 orders the work and makes the calibration blunt: **stages 0–5 are all wanted for reasons unrelated to ray tracing** (Vulkan for consoles, the render scene for culling, F0 for metals, scene colour for water, motion vectors for anything temporal), and stage 6–7 is the part Capcom staffed with **two developers for 1.5 years**. §5 is what is reachable *today* and it is more than it sounds: **this project already has an acceleration structure and it beats a BVH for the geometry it covers** — a 24×24 lattice summarised to two bytes per cell in `OcclusionGrid`, cache-resident whole, with a 3D DDA already written in `RayCaster`, so a voxel tracer in compute needs no BVH, no Vulkan and no RT hardware, and buys sun visibility beyond the frustum, sky occlusion and cone-traced soft shadows. Its limit is stated first rather than discovered: the lattice describes *architecture*, not *render geometry*, so props, unit bodies, ramps and every `kNeedsTile` cell are invisible to it and voxel shadows read wrong exactly where the eye looks — at contact. §6's ranking lands on the same order [`re_engine_rendering.md`](games/rendering/re_engine_rendering.md) §11 reached from the opposite direction, which is the reason to trust it: **local lights, then SSR, then probe GI, then a voxel visibility term, then Vulkan when consoles force it, then ray tracing if ever**. The uncomfortable framing is the useful one — the gap between this renderer's frame and RE4R's is not a technique the engine cannot reach, and RE4R ships *reflections only*. |


## Where the XCOM 2 material came from

Unlike From Dust, none of this is guesswork from a disassembly. UE3 ships its materials as
serialised node graphs inside the package, so the graph *is* recoverable — the SDK's packages
are uncompressed and the name, import and export tables read straight out:

```
XCOM 2 SDK/XComGame/Content/XCOM_2/Packages/UI/UI_3D.upk
  -> Tile.MovementBorder                      (UE3 package version 845, licensee 105)
```

which is the material the game is told to use, in
`XCOM 2 SDK/XComGame/Config/XComEngine.ini`:

```
MovementGridMaterialName=UI_3D.Tile.MovementBorder
MovementGridDotsMaterialName=UI_3D.Tile.MovementTile
```

Every constant, connection and material flag in the HLSL is transcribed from the
`MaterialExpression` objects under that material. The two `MaterialExpressionCustom` bodies are
literal — they are the only hand-written HLSL that ships inside a UE3 material, and they are
quoted exactly:

```hlsl
return 1.0-saturate((WorldPos.z - (FadeHeight-FadeDistance))/ FadeDistance);   // "Height Fading"
```

Node defaults that the package leaves unserialised were read from the SDK's own UnrealScript,
`Development/SrcOrig/Engine/Classes/MaterialExpression*.uc` — which matters more than it sounds:
`MaterialExpressionConstantScale` defaults its `Scale` to **0.5**, not 1.

Two more sources fill in the parts the material does not own:

- `XComGame/Config/XComGame.ini`, `[XComGame.XComMovementGridComponent]` — the ribbon's width,
  height offset, length factor and UV tiling distance. These shape the mesh, and the material's
  UVs only mean anything alongside them.
- `XComEngineMaterials.upk` → `MovementGrid_Material` — the engine-default fallback. A different,
  older take on the same idea, and worth reading against the shipping one: it drives its emissive
  from a cursor-proximity glow and reverses the depth comparison. Its differences are noted inline
  in the HLSL.

## What is *not* recovered

`MovementBorder_Line`, the 64×64 BC5 two-channel texture holding the line's cross-section
profiles. The port reconstructs both channels analytically in
[`../assets/shaders/ribbon.fs.glsl`](../src/cromwell/assets/shaders/ribbon.fs.glsl) rather than shipping the
game's asset. That is the single deviation, and it is a deviation of asset, not of maths.
